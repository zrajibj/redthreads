## What Red Threads is

Red Threads is an educational channel on YouTube, Facebook and Instagram. It sets out to answer one question for people everywhere: **why does changing the government change so little?**

In country after country, power changes hands and hope rises, and before long the same complaints return: corruption, prices, wages that don't keep up. The channel's answer, drawn from classic books by Engels, Marx, Einstein, Rosa Luxemburg and others, is that the problem sits in the base of society: who owns the land, the factories and the wealth, who does the work, and who keeps the surplus. Change the faces at the top without changing the base, and the building stays the same. The tagline is "The base, not the face."

A few things matter about how the channel works:

- **It is educational, not partisan.** It never attacks the current government or any past one, never tells viewers which party to support, and never calls people onto the streets. It explains the structure that every government inherits.
- **It is global, with Bangladesh as home.** Each lesson is made in English, with a Bangla version from the same animation.
- **The format is short animated lessons** in the style of TED-Ed: 4 to 6 minutes on YouTube and Facebook, with 30 to 60 second clips for Reels and Shorts.
- **Every lesson is sourced.** Quotes come from the books, and the book, chapter and page go in the description.

The first lesson, "Why does changing the government change so little?", already has an English and a Bangla first cut, which I can send you to watch.

## Two ways to do it

### Option A: a HeyGen avatar of you (preferred)

HeyGen is a service that builds a video "digital twin" of a real person from a few minutes of footage. Once your avatar exists, I can type an approved script and HeyGen produces a video of you saying it, with your face and your voice. HeyGen can also produce the same clip in other languages, so one recording could give us both the English and the Bangla intro. We would test how natural the Bangla sounds before relying on it.

**What you would record, once:**

- **Training footage.** Two to five minutes of you talking naturally to the camera, in one continuous take: good light on your face, a quiet room, the camera steady at eye level, and you looking into the lens. You can talk about anything; what matters is natural speech and expression. HeyGen recommends short pauses with your mouth closed and only gentle hand movements. A good phone on a tripod is enough.
- **A consent video.** HeyGen will only build an avatar of a real person if that person agrees on camera. You read a short consent statement that HeyGen provides, saying that you allow HeyGen to use your footage to create an avatar. HeyGen checks that the face in the consent video matches the training footage. Nobody can create an avatar of you without this step, which also means the avatar cannot exist without your explicit agreement.
- **Voice.** HeyGen can clone your voice from the training footage. If we want a cleaner voice, you might also read a short passage in a quiet room.

**Pros:** about two hours of your time to set up, then only reviewing scripts. Easy to make the Bangla and English versions match. New intros and outros can be made without booking you each time.

**Cons:** an avatar is close to real but not identical, and some viewers notice. Platforms ask creators to disclose realistic AI-generated video of a person, so every video using the avatar would say so (see the next section). I would also confirm before we start that HeyGen's usage rules allow educational content on political and economic ideas.

### Option B: raw video you record yourself

You record each intro and outro yourself, on a phone or camera, from a script we agree. I handle the editing.

**Pros:** completely real, which builds the most trust. No AI involved and nothing to disclose.

**Cons:** more of your time, roughly 30 to 60 minutes for each lesson's intro and outro in one language, and more again for a second language. The schedule depends on both of us being free.

### A mix (my suggestion)

Use the avatar for the routine intros and outros, and record yourself for the pieces where being real matters most: the channel trailer, the welcome video and replies to viewers.
